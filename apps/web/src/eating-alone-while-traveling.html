Eating alone while traveling, and how to find dinner company

Eating alone while traveling is normal, and often the best way to eat well. If you'd rather have company tonight, the quickest routes are a bar seat or communal table, a food tour or cooking class, your hostel's dinner, or a dinner plan you join or post in an app. Here's how each one works, and how to stay safe when the company is strangers.

Do you want a good meal or good company?

Two different problems get mixed up here. If you just don't want to feel awkward, you need tactics for a table for one. If you're lonely, a better seat won't fix it: you need to be where other people are eating. Work out which one it is tonight, then use the matching section below.

You're not unusual either way. OpenTable's data, reported in July 2026, shows reservations for one up 23% over the past 12 months.

When eating alone is the better choice

  • The place is the point. A ramen counter, a tasting bar, a market stall with a long queue. Nobody has to compromise, and a single seat is easier to get than a table.
  • You're tired or jet-lagged. Eat early, order what you want and be in bed by nine, with nobody to be polite to.
  • You have food rules. Allergies, a diet or a low spice limit are easier to manage when you're the only one ordering.
  • You want to see how a place works. A counter seat gives you a view of the kitchen and a chance to ask the staff what's good.

Make a table for one easy

  1. Take the counter or the bar. It's built for people on their own, and talking to the staff or the person beside you is normal. What goes wrong: some bars only serve drinks, so check you can order food there.
  2. Go just before the dinner rush. Tables are easier to get and the staff have time for you. If a place takes bookings, message or call and book for one.
  3. Make lunch your big meal. Fixed-price lunch menus, such as the menú del día in Spain or teishoku sets in Japan, come as one portion and are easy to order alone.
  4. Pick food that comes in single portions. Noodle shops, street food, food halls and night markets need no explanation and no table. Some barbecue and hot pot places want a two-portion minimum, so ask before you sit. Korean barbecue is the classic case: our Seoul guide covers a food culture built around sharing.
  5. Save a short list before you leave. Two or three places per neighborhood in your maps app means you aren't walking hungry at 8pm and choosing the first place with a free table.
  6. Put your phone away for the first few minutes. A book or notebook is fine, but people talk to someone who looks up.
  7. Use the food to start a conversation. "What's that you're having?" works at every counter. If you can't read the menu, point at a neighbor's dish or use a translation app's camera.

How to find dinner company, from least to most effort

These put you at a table with other people. Most work best if you start in the afternoon, not at 7pm.

  1. A communal table or chef's counter. Some restaurants, food halls and beer halls seat strangers together. There's nothing to plan: sit down and say hello.
  2. Your hostel's dinner. Many hostels cook a communal meal or run a family dinner night. Ask at the desk what's on tonight and who's going. Some hostels let non-guests join, so ask. Hostelworld's app lists events in each city, and its App Store listing says Social Pass opens city chats and meetups even without a hostel booking. What goes wrong: private rooms and quiet hostels often skip events, so check the events board before you book.
  3. A food tour or cooking class. You spend a few hours with the same group and the meal is the point. Ask the guide whether people usually eat together at the end. Our guide to meeting people while traveling solo covers more of these.
  4. A dinner hosted by locals. EatWith lists dinners, food tours and cooking classes hosted by locals, which you book by city. They're scheduled events, so look a few days ahead.
  5. A matched dinner with strangers. Timeleft books you onto a table of 4 to 6 people matched by personality, interests and age. Its App Store listing says dinners run every Wednesday and women-only dinners every Tuesday at 7pm, and you find out the venue and get a preview of who you'll meet on the day. Its site says it runs in 52 countries and that you pay for your own food and drinks at the venue. The US App Store lists its in-app purchases at $19.99, $46.99 and $62.99. It suits you if you're in town on those nights and want someone else to arrange everything. See how Timeleft compares with Tablamigo.
  6. A Meetup group. Meetup lets you browse groups by city and topic. Try searching for food, supper clubs or foodies. Groups are mostly local and events are usually posted days ahead, so look before you land, and check each event's cost.
  7. A plan app. Tablamigo and Nomadtable are built around plans: someone posts "street food at 7" and others nearby join. Nomadtable's listing says you can meet up for anything from dinner to sightseeing. It's been around longer and has more than 20,000 App Store ratings, while Tablamigo is new. Staying safe at dinner

On your own:

  • Know how you're getting back before the second drink, and book your own ride if you need one.
  • Watch your drink and know your limits.
  • Where phone snatching is common, don't leave your phone on a street-side table.

With people you've just met:

  • Meet in a busy public restaurant, not a private home or hotel room.
  • Keep the chat in the app until you've met, so you can report anything that goes wrong.
  • Tell a friend where you're going and who with.
  • Pay your own share. Never send money to someone you've just met.
  • Leave whenever you want. You don't owe anyone an explanation.

Questions

Is it weird to eat alone at a restaurant while traveling?

No. OpenTable's data shows reservations for one up 23% over the past 12 months, and counters, food halls, markets and noodle shops are full of people eating alone. If you want to feel less on display, take a counter seat or a casual place.

How do I find someone to have dinner with while traveling?

Put yourself where other people are eating: a hostel dinner, a food tour, a cooking class, a communal table, a Meetup, a matched dinner like Timeleft or a plan in an app like Tablamigo or Nomadtable. Start in the afternoon, because the same evening is easier to arrange than the same hour.

Is there an app to find dinner companions while traveling?

Yes. Timeleft matches you with a table of 4 to 6 strangers, Nomadtable and Tablamigo let you join or post dinner plans, and Meetup lists group events. Tablamigo is new, so in many cities you may be the first to post a plan.

How do I eat alone without feeling awkward?

Take a counter or bar seat, go just before the dinner rush, and start with lunch if dinner feels like too much. Put your phone away for a few minutes and ask a neighbor what they're eating.

Is it safe to have dinner with strangers from an app?

Most people are just travelers looking for company, but take the usual care: meet in a busy public restaurant, keep the chat in the app until you've met, tell a friend where you're going, pay only your own share and get yourself home. Look at profiles first and report anything that feels off.

Can I join a hostel dinner if I'm not staying there?

Sometimes. Some hostels let outsiders join dinners and events and some don't, so ask at the desk or message ahead. If you're booking a hostel for the dinners, check its events board first.
