How to meet people in hostels: 12 things that work

Pick a hostel with a busy common room and a kitchen, go and sit in it before you unpack, say hello to your dorm mates and go to whatever the hostel has on tonight. Most hostel friendships start with one of those, and the hostel you choose matters more than anything you say.

Pick the hostel before you pick the bed

Whether you meet people is mostly decided at booking. Check these before you pay:

  • Read what guests say about the atmosphere. Search the reviews for "social", "met people" and "atmosphere". Hostelworld's listing says its ratings cover value, location and atmosphere, so look at the atmosphere score, not only the overall one.
  • Look for shared space. A bar, guest kitchen, rooftop or courtyard puts guests in one place together. A corridor of bunks doesn't.
  • Check the events. Family dinners, quiz nights, pub crawls and walking tours seat strangers together. If the page doesn't list them, ask the hostel.
  • Dorm or private room? A dorm of four to eight beds gives you roommates to talk to every day, and very large dorms are livelier and louder. A private room in a social hostel works too, because you meet people in the common room and kitchen, not in bed. A private room in a quiet hostel is the combination to avoid.

12 things that work

  1. Go to the common room before you unpack. Groups form on the first evening, and it's easier to join one early than on day three. Lock your bag away and sit somewhere shared for an hour. What goes wrong: "recharging" on your bunk until tomorrow.
  2. Say hi to your dorm mates when you arrive. "Hi, just got in. How long have you been here?" is enough. You're sharing a room anyway, so it's the lowest-risk first conversation.
  3. Use the kitchen. Cooking next to strangers invites comments. Ask what someone's making, offer your extra pasta, or wash up beside someone.
  4. Eat breakfast in the common room. If it's included, take a table with a free seat. Everyone is awake, hungry and not yet in a group for the day.
  5. Go to the hostel's own events, even the cheesy ones. At a quiz night or family dinner everyone is there to meet people. What goes wrong: a pub crawl of 40 splits into friend groups, so a dinner or a tour is an easier start.
  6. Ask the front desk. Staff know what's on and who is heading where. "Is anyone else going to the waterfall tomorrow?" is a fair question.
  7. Join the hostel chat before you arrive. If you book through Hostelworld, the app has chats for the people staying at your hostel, so you can say hello before you check in. What goes wrong: a chat is only as busy as its guests make it.
  8. Keep a deck of cards in your bag. Shuffling at a common-room table draws players over without you asking anyone.
  9. Ask where people are going next. Overlapping routes give you a reason to travel together or meet again two cities on.
  10. Say yes to the first group plan. When a group decides to get food or head to the beach, go, even if you're tired. What goes wrong: "maybe later". Later rarely comes.
  11. Post your own plan. "Rooftop dinner, 8pm, four spots" on the notice board, in the hostel chat or in an app gets more takers than "anyone want to hang out?". Give a time, a place and a number.
  12. Stay two or three nights, not one. Friendships need repeat contact: the person from dinner is at breakfast tomorrow. A new hostel every night resets you to zero.

If you're shy or an introvert

  • Ask to join instead of starting something. "Mind if I sit here?" takes less effort than opening a conversation, and works as well.
  • Pick small events and aim for one real conversation a night. A dinner for six is easier than a party for forty.
  • Give yourself a job. Cooking or dealing cards gives your hands something to do and others a reason to talk to you.
  • Set a stop time, and take quiet evenings. "I'll stay an hour" is easier to start.

When the hostel is quiet

Sometimes the common room is full of people on laptops. Take the same effort outside: a free walking tour, a group day trip, a class or a plan from an app. More in how to meet people while traveling solo. Heading to India? See hostels in India below.

Hostels in India

A few hostel chains have hostels in most of India's backpacker towns, and each says something different about its social side. What their own sites and listings said on 11 October 2026:

  • Zostel calls itself a backpacker hostel and social travel brand. Its hostel pages list daily activities (Zostel Gokarna plans them two weeks ahead), and several list female-only dorms, as in Gokarna and Hampi. Its app, "Zostel – Zo Trips", also sells group trips and has a "Meet New People" line: "Travel with strangers, leave with friends" (Google Play listing, updated 9 October 2026).
  • The Hosteller lists a common area and a café at the hostels we checked, sells local tours at some (in Bir: evening walks, camping nights and cycling trips), and says in each hostel's FAQ whether it has female-only dorms.
  • goSTOPS describes hostels like its Bir one, by the paragliding landing site, as a space to meet new travelers, with a garden, a bonfire and a barbecue. Several list female-only dorms, as in Goa, Rishikesh, McLeod Ganj and Delhi.
  • Madpackers has hostels in Kasol, Manali, Bir, Rishikesh, Varanasi, Jaipur, Jodhpur, Udaipur, Amritsar and Anjuna in Goa, and promises "a calendar full of treks, jam nights and shared dinners", with female and mixed dorms.

Staying safe in a hostel

  • Lock your valuables. Bring your own padlock, since not every hostel supplies one, and keep your passport and cards in the locker when you go out.
  • Look after your drink on pub crawls. Set your limit before you start, keep your drink in your hand and go home with people you came with.
  • Meet new people in shared spaces first. Don't follow someone to a private room on day one.
  • Women can choose a female-only dorm. Hostelworld lists mixed and female-only options, and Tablamigo has women-only plans that only women can see and join. See our solo female travel guide.
  • Tell someone where you're going, never lend money to people you've just met, and keep chats in the app until you've met in person. More in our safety guide.

Questions

How do you meet people in a hostel?

Choose a hostel with a common room, a guest kitchen and regular events, then use them. Sit in the common room in your first hour, say hello to your dorm mates, go to the hostel's dinner or tour, and suggest something specific, like dinner at 8, rather than "see you around".

How do I make friends in a hostel if I'm shy?

Go to small, structured events, ask to join a table instead of starting a conversation, and aim for one good chat a night. Cards or cooking give you something to do while people get comfortable. Set a stop time and take quiet evenings when you need them.

Is there an app to meet people in hostels?

Yes. Hostelworld's app has Hostel Chats for guests staying at the same hostel when you book through it, and a paid Social Pass for city chats without a booking. Tablamigo and Nomadtable show plans from travelers nearby wherever you're staying, so they also work if your hostel is quiet.

Is it better to stay in a dorm or a private room to meet people?

A dorm of four to eight beds gives you built-in conversation with your roommates. A private room in a hostel with a busy common room works too. A private room in a quiet hostel is the hardest way to meet anyone.

Is it safe to stay in a hostel alone?

Many solo travelers do. Pick a hostel with good recent reviews, bring a padlock, watch your drink on pub crawls and meet new people in shared spaces first. Women can choose female-only dorms where offered.

Which hostels in India are good for solo travelers?

The big chains are Zostel, The Hosteller, goSTOPS and Madpackers, with hostels in most backpacker towns. Our India city guides list the ones we checked in each town, with what each offers and which have female-only dorms.

Can I join a hostel's events if I'm not staying there?

Some hostels let outsiders join dinners, tours and bar nights, and some keep them for guests. Ask at reception first.
