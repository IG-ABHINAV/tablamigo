Solo travel in India: where to go and meet other travelers

India is easier to travel alone than its reputation suggests, and far easier with company. Solo travelers, Indian and foreign, cluster on a few well-worn routes, so knowing where they are is most of the battle. Here are the places, the months, where to start a first solo trip, how to plan it and how to find people to travel with.

Where solo travelers gather in India

Most travelers move along a handful of circuits. These are the places where you're most likely to find other people traveling alone, and each has its own guide with hostels and meeting spots we checked.

The Himalayas: Himachal, Uttarakhand, Ladakh and Spiti

  • Manali: Old Manali's cafés and guesthouses, hot springs in Vashisht and treks up the valley.
  • Kasol: the Parvati Valley's backpacker village and the start of the Kheerganga trek.
  • McLeod Ganj: Tibetan culture, meditation courses and the Triund trek above Dharamshala.
  • Bir Billing: paragliding from September, a Tibetan colony and cafés around the landing site, 50 km from Dharamshala.
  • Rishikesh: yoga, rafting and cafés on the Ganges.
  • Leh-Ladakh: the base for Ladakh's lakes and passes, where sharing a taxi is half the reason to meet people.
  • Spiti Valley: Kaza is the base, reached through Kinnaur from Shimla or over the Kunzum Pass from Manali. Tourist shared taxis are scarce, so travelers team up to share one.

The plains and Rajasthan

  • Delhi: where many trips start, with Old Delhi's food lanes and buses north to the mountains.
  • Jaipur: forts, palaces and bazaars, four to five hours from Delhi.
  • Udaipur: lakes, palaces and rooftops where travelers meet at sunset.
  • Varanasi: the ghats, the evening aarti and the old city's lanes.

The south and the coast

  • Goa: beaches, shacks and a big backpacker and nomad scene in the north.
  • Gokarna: a temple town on the Karnataka coast with a run of beaches you can walk between.
  • Hampi: ruins and boulders explored by bicycle, with sunsets on the hills.

Your first solo trip in India: where to start

This is our judgement, and here's the reasoning. A first solo trip is easiest where there are lots of other travelers, several hostels built around meeting people, and short distances, so you're never far from company or a change of plan. By that measure, three places stand out:

  • Rishikesh: our guide lists three social hostels in Tapovan and near Lakshman Jhula, and the evening aarti and drop-in yoga classes give you something to join on day one. The town is vegetarian and alcohol isn't sold in the centre, so it's calm.
  • North Goa, from November to February: hostels in Morjim, Anjuna and Vagator, the Arambol sunset drum circle and the Wednesday flea market in Anjuna. Distances are long, so plan on a scooter or a taxi app.
  • McLeod Ganj: three hostels, drop-in meditation at Tushita and the Triund trek, which people usually do in small groups. Meditation and cooking courses are an easy way to meet people staying a while.

Varanasi and Delhi are more intense for a first trip: big crowds, touts at the stations and lanes that are hard to find your way around after dark. They're very doable, just easier once you've traveled alone before. We'd also leave Ladakh's and Spiti's altitude, and the Parvati Valley's longer treks, until you've done a trip or two, or go with a group.

Planning the trip

  1. Pick one region and one season. The table above keeps you out of the monsoon and the heat. Two or three bases in a week is plenty.
  2. Book the long legs first. Trains fill up around holidays and weekends, so book on IRCTC, Indian Railways' own booking site, as early as you can. Indian Railways keeps some coaches and berths for women, so check the options when you book.
  3. Arrive in daylight when you can, and book your first night before you land. The UK government's travel advice for India says to avoid road travel at night, when accidents and crime are more common, and not to ride alone in, or hail, taxis on the street, especially at night.
  4. The overnight bus to the hills. Buses for Kasol leave Delhi's ISBT Kashmere Gate, and buses for Dharamshala and McLeod Ganj also pick up at Majnu ka Tilla. An evening departure arrives early the next morning, so it is road travel at night. Our view: if you take the overnight bus anyway, book a known operator and have a bed booked for when you arrive.
  5. Book a social hostel for the first two nights. Each of our guides lists hostels we checked on their own websites. Two nights in one place gives you time to meet people before you move on.
  6. Sort your phone and money. Get an Indian SIM with data, and check it works in Ladakh, where some connections from other states don't. Carry cash for the hills: in Spiti, Kaza's SBI ATM is often out of service.
  7. Tell someone your route, and save your hostels on an offline map.

How to find travel companions in India

  1. Stay in hostels on the backpacker routes. India now has several hostel chains and many independent hostels in most towns on this page. Common rooms and group dinners are the easiest place to start.
  2. Join a group trek, activity or trip. Treks like Triund, Kheerganga and the Himalayan day hikes are often done in small groups, and so are rafting in Rishikesh and yoga or meditation courses. Operators also run weekend group trips from the big cities; solo trips with strangers in India compares them with hostel-run trips and going alone.
  3. Use the overnight bus. Everyone on the Delhi buses to Kasol, Manali and McLeod Ganj is heading the same way. Read the note on night travel under "Planning the trip" first.
  4. Post a plan on Tablamigo. A specific plan, like "sunset at Hemakuta Hill, 5:30" or "sharing a taxi to Pangong on Friday", lets travelers nearby join you. Add your next city as a trip to see who'll be there on your dates.
  5. Look in city and travel groups. Local Facebook groups and hostel WhatsApp groups are active in most of these places.

Safety for solo travelers in India

  • Emergency numbers: 112 is India's national emergency number, and 181 is the Women Helpline.
  • Getting around: use ride-hailing apps, prepaid taxi counters at airports and stations, or the metro where there is one.
  • Meeting people: meet in busy public places, keep chats in the app until you're comfortable, and tell a friend where you're going.
  • Scams: ignore anyone at a railway station who says your ticket or hotel is invalid, and never buy gems or goods "to resell at home".
  • Drugs: cannabis and other drugs are illegal in India, including in the hill towns where they're openly offered.
  • Altitude: in Leh and Spiti, rest for the first two days after you arrive, especially if you fly in.
  • Women traveling alone: the UK government's advice says women travelers often face harassment, and to avoid isolated places, including beaches, when alone. Questions

Where should I go for my first solo trip in India?

In our judgement, Rishikesh, North Goa from November to February, or McLeod Ganj. Each has several social hostels, short distances and things to join on your first day, like the evening aarti in Rishikesh or drop-in meditation in McLeod Ganj. Varanasi and Delhi are more intense for a first trip.

Can I do a solo trip with strangers in India?

Yes, in three ways: book a group trip with an operator, stay in hostels that run their own trips, or go alone and meet people when you get there. Our page on solo trips with strangers in India compares them, with prices and a checklist for vetting an operator.

Is India safe to travel alone?

The UK government's travel advice for India, updated on 7 October 2026, warns about road accidents and crime at night, scams in tourist areas, spiked food and drinks, and harassment of women. Its advice includes avoiding road travel at night, using app or prepaid taxis instead of hailing one on the street, and not accepting food or drinks from strangers. The safety tips above and each city guide's own tips cover the rest.

What is the best app in India to find a travel partner?

It depends on what you want. For company on the day, like a meal, a trek or a shared taxi, an app where travelers post plans works best: Tablamigo does this, works on Android and in any browser in India, and is free to use. For planning a whole trip together months ahead, trip-matching apps and travel groups fit better. Our comparison of travel buddy apps in India covers the options.

Where can I go for a solo trip in India in July or August?

Ladakh is the usual pick, since July and August are its busiest months and the monsoon hits most of the rest of India. Spiti's road from Manali usually opens between late May and mid-June. Check road conditions first.

Is Nomadtable available in India?

On Android yes, on iPhone no; our comparison of travel buddy apps in India has the details.

Which places in India are best for solo travelers to meet people?

Goa, Rishikesh, Manali, Kasol, McLeod Ganj, Varanasi, Udaipur, Hampi and Leh all have large numbers of solo travelers in season, along with hostels and cafés built around them. Spiti, Gokarna and Bir are smaller, with fewer hostels.
