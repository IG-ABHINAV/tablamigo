How to meet people while traveling solo

Join something that already has people in it. A hostel dinner, a free walking tour, a group day trip, a class or a plan in an app all hand you a reason to talk. Waiting for strangers to start a conversation almost never works.

10 ways that work

  1. Go to the hostel's events, even if you're not sleeping there. Family dinners, pub crawls and quiz nights exist to mix strangers. Many hostels let outsiders join events or use the bar. What goes wrong: private rooms and quiet "boutique" hostels often skip events, so check the events board before you book. More in how to meet people in hostels.
  2. Take a free walking tour on your first morning. They usually run on tips, take two to three hours and finish in the center, near places to eat. Ask whoever you walked next to if they want to eat. What goes wrong: tours of 30+ people break into couples and friend groups; smaller evening tours are easier.
  3. Book group day trips. A boat trip, a volcano hike or a cooking class puts you with the same eight people for a day. By lunch you have plans for the evening. What goes wrong: private tours are more comfortable and completely unsocial.
  4. Use an app that starts from plans, not profiles. Open Tablamigo or Nomadtable, see who's getting dinner or going for a hike nearby, and join. What goes wrong: chatting for days before meeting. Propose a time and place by the second or third message. Our roundup of apps to meet travelers compares the options.
  5. Post your own plan. "Street food in Chinatown, 7pm, anyone?" gets more replies than "anyone want to hang out?" Be specific about the place and time. What goes wrong: posting for "sometime this week". Nobody commits to that.
  6. Join a language exchange or Meetup group. These pull in locals as well as travelers, and they meet at the same bar every week. What goes wrong: groups are often scheduled days ahead, so look before you arrive.
  7. Work from a coworking space if you're a digital nomad. Many run community lunches, workshops or after-work drinks, so ask at the desk. See how to find digital nomad meetups.
  8. Take a class. Surfing, salsa, Muay Thai, cooking. You'll see the same faces for a few days, which is how travel friendships form.
  9. Sit at the bar, not a table. Counters, shared tables at food halls and communal tables at hostels make talking to your neighbor normal. For more on meals, see eating alone while traveling.
  10. Say yes, then say where. When someone mentions they're going to the night market, ask if you can come. Other travelers usually say yes.

What to say first

You don't need a line. Ask something practical: "Have you been to the market yet?", "Is this the queue for the boat?", "What's good here?" The follow-up is always "How long are you here for?", and then you can suggest doing something. Travelers expect this. It's the easiest social setting most people will ever be in.

If you're shy or an introvert

  • Choose structured things: tours, classes and group trips, where the activity does the talking.
  • Pick small groups. A dinner for four is easier than a pub crawl for forty.
  • Set a stop time. "I'll go for an hour" is easier to start, and you can always stay.
  • Take rest days. Meeting people every night is tiring; nobody's keeping score.

When solo travel gets lonely

For a lot of people it hits a few days in, once the excitement of arriving wears off, or on the first night in a new city. Act on it early. Book one social thing for tomorrow: a walking tour in the morning or a group dinner at night. Call someone at home, then go somewhere with people in it, even if it's just a busy café. Staying in your room with your phone makes it worse.

Backpacker and traveler meetups

Meetups for travelers do exist, but they're scattered across several sites, and many listings are years old. These are the places they're posted:

  • Meetup groups. Search for "travel", "backpackers" or "solo travel" plus the city. Open the group's past events before you join: plenty of big groups haven't met in months.
  • Luma and AllEvents. Event sites where communities post one-off meetups, including traveler meetups like Niyo's below. Search by city.
  • Hostel event boards. Usually the most current listing, because it's for today and tomorrow. Many hostels let outsiders join, so ask at reception.
  • Couchsurfing events. Couchsurfing lists events, but its blog says you need a paid membership to use Events and Hangouts. Our Couchsurfing Hangouts comparison has the details.
  • Nomads.com for digital nomads. A good example of why dates matter: its Goa page showed one meetup ever, in January 2023, when we checked on 11 October 2026.

Whatever the site, look at the date of the last event that actually happened, not the member count.

In India, Niyo's travel community has run traveler meetups in Bengaluru, Mumbai, Pune and Delhi since 2024, announced in its forum and on Luma; the latest one we found, when we checked on 11 October 2026, was in Mumbai on 8 November 2025. Hostel chains such as Zostel, The Hosteller and goSTOPS are where most backpackers meet on the road. Staying safe while you meet people

  • Meet in busy public places for a first meetup, not a private home or hotel room.
  • Keep chats in the app until you've met, so you can report anything that goes wrong.
  • Tell a friend where you're going, and get yourself there and back.
  • Never send money to someone you've just met.

Questions

How can I make friends while traveling solo?

Do things with other people instead of waiting to be approached: hostel events, free walking tours, group day trips, classes and plans in apps like Tablamigo. Suggest a specific next step, like dinner at 7, rather than swapping numbers for "sometime".

What is the best app to meet people while traveling?

For meeting other travelers the same day, a plan-based app like Nomadtable or Tablamigo works best. Hostelworld is good if you stay in hostels, and Meetup if you want locals. Our roundup of the best apps to meet travelers compares them.

How do I deal with loneliness while traveling alone?

Act on it early. Book one social activity for the next day, like a walking tour or a group dinner, go somewhere busy instead of staying in your room, and call someone at home.

Is it hard to meet people when traveling alone?

It's easier than at home, because other solo travelers are looking for company too. It's hardest in private rooms, resort areas and small towns, so put yourself where travelers gather: hostels, tours, classes and busy food areas.

Where can I find backpacker meetups?

On Meetup, Luma and AllEvents, by searching for travel or backpacker meetups in your city, on hostel event boards, and, for digital nomads, on Nomads.com. Check when each group last actually met, because many listings are stale. Or post your own plan on an app like Tablamigo.

Is it safe to meet strangers while traveling?

Most are just travelers looking for company, but take the same care you would anywhere: meet in busy public places, keep chats in the app, tell a friend where you're going and trust your instincts.
